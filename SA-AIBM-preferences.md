### Preference Chips (Response Style)

**Corporate Secretary**
- Concise and structured — "Keep answers concise, structured, and easy to scan."
- Risks and deadlines first — "Lead with risks, deadlines, missing items, and blockers."
- Checklist format — "Use checklists and clear next steps when helpful."
- Use checklists for reviews — "Use checklist format for readiness reviews, action tracking, and to-do items."
- Identify action owners — "Where possible, identify who is responsible for each action or decision."
- Include key dates — "Highlight key deadlines, reporting dates, and milestone timelines."

**Board Administrator**

- Short summaries — "Prefer short summaries over long explanations."
- Action-oriented — "Keep responses direct, operational, and action-oriented."
- Use checklists for reviews — "Use checklist format for readiness reviews, action tracking, and to-do items."
- Identify action owners — "Where possible, identify who is responsible for each action or decision."
- Include key dates — "Highlight key deadlines, reporting dates, and milestone timelines."

- Include regulatory context — "Reference relevant regulatory frameworks and compliance implications when discussing governance topics."
- Note governance best practice — "Where relevant, note applicable governance best practice or regulatory guidance."
- Remind me to verify high-stakes items — "Add a verification reminder when discussing regulatory filings, legal matters, or board votes."

- Include historical context — "When relevant, include historical context from previous meetings or decisions on the same topic."
- Include regulatory context — "Reference relevant regulatory frameworks and compliance implications when discussing governance topics."
- Identify action owners — "Where possible, identify who is responsible for each action or decision."

**Executive Assistant**
- Plain language — "Avoid jargon and explain technical terms when used."
- Brief and actionable — "Keep answers brief and highly actionable."
- Step by step — "Use simple step-by-step guidance when giving instructions."
- Use checklists for reviews — "Use checklist format for readiness reviews, action tracking, and to-do items."
- Include key dates — "Highlight key deadlines, reporting dates, and milestone timelines."
- Identify action owners — "Where possible, identify who is responsible for each action or decision."

### Suggestion Chips (Composer / Home Hero)

All Smart Assist (admin) users see the same chip set:

- **Find materials**
  - "Find materials related to [topic] from last year."
  - "Which board and committee packs reference [topic / document] since [year]?"
  - "Locate the latest version of [policy / charter / report] and where it was last circulated."

- **Trace decisions**
  - "When was [initiative / policy] formally approved?"
  - "Which committee approved [decision], and what was recorded in the minutes?"
  - "Show the approval trail for [initiative] — from first tabled to final sign-off."

- **Answer questions**
  - "Has the board previously discussed [topic]?"
  - "What did the board conclude the last time [topic] was raised?"
  - "Which committee or directors have ownership of [topic / action]?"

- **Onboard director**
  - "Find induction materials and recent board context."
  - "What are the key decisions and approvals from the past year a new director should know?"
  - "Which committee papers and policies should a new director read before their first [committee] meeting?"

### Preference Chip Texts by Role

All director roles share the same 5 chip labels; only "Lead with the risks" and (for Board chair / CEO) "Keep responses under 200 words" have role-specific text:

- **Lead with the risks**
  - Director / Non-exec: "Lead with the key risks, open issues, and decisions I should pay attention to."
  - Independent director: "Lead with the risks, tradeoffs, and unresolved concerns."
  - Committee chair: "Lead with the biggest issues, decisions, and escalation points."
  - Board chair: "Lead with the most material risks and decisions for the board."
  - CEO: "Lead with strategic risks, implications, and actions."
  - CFO: "Lead with financial risks, controls issues, and key implications."
  - COO: "Lead with operational risks, bottlenecks, and next steps."

- **Short answer first** *(all roles, same text)*
  - "Lead with the direct answer in one or two sentences, then supporting detail below."

- **Tables for comparisons** *(all roles, same text)*
  - "When comparing items, periods, or committees, use a table rather than bullets."

- **Keep it to 3–5 bullets** *(all roles, same text)*
  - "Keep responses to three to five bullets. Use full sentences, not fragments."

- **Keep responses under 200 words**
  - Director / Non-exec / Independent / Committee chair / CFO / COO: "Keep responses under 200 words unless more detail is necessary."
  - Board chair: "Keep responses under 200 words and focus on what matters most."
  - CEO: "Keep responses under 200 words unless I ask for more depth."

### Suggestion Chips (Composer / Home Hero)

All AIBM (director) users see the same chip set:

- **Find topics**
  - "When did the board approve [policy], and what was resolved?"
  - "Where has [topic] been discussed across recent board and committee materials?"
  - "Has the board considered [proposal / topic] before, and what was the outcome?"

- **Prepare for meetings**
  - "What should I review before [committee] based on last meeting's follow-ups?"
  - "What action items are still open going into the next [committee] meeting?"
  - "What are the key decisions and risks I should be ready to discuss at [committee]?"

- **Monitor trends**
  - "How has [risk / metric / topic] changed across the last [X] board cycles?"
  - "Is [risk — e.g. cyber / liquidity / credit] trending better or worse across recent executive reports, and why?"
  - "How has [financial metric / KPI] moved over the last [X] quarters relative to plan?"

- **Trace decisions**
  - "How has the board's view on [topic] changed over the last year?"
  - "What was the board's rationale for [decision], and were any concerns or dissent recorded?"
  - "Trace the history of [initiative] — when it was first raised, discussed, and approved."
